## What we know from the founder

- Bronze Mission 1: **four qualifying paid-and-collected purchased kota units**, even in one order on one day; exactly one first free eligible kota (maximum proposed R25 per eligible admitted customer), then permanent Silver rank. No hidden forced visit/different-day rule.
- Founder's next objective: a goal customers willingly complete and a business investors can trust. **True separate paid return orders** are the outcome metric; collecting ten extra units in one basket is not ten visits.
- Planned full-ladder Gold benefit: **R80 restricted promotional food credit** awarded on reaching Gold, potentially spendable in parts across eligible participating shops, with any customer-funded shortfall paid at the physical shop. Gold benefit is **not live** and **not offered in the accepted 12-person R500 Bronze-only pilot**.
- Platinum's planned R150 credit and the number of Gold-to-Platinum qualifying units remain separate open decisions.
- All food amounts below use an **illustrative R25 per purchased kota**, not a verified average selling price or a statement of LocalEats revenue. The company pays reward costs, but the shop receives the customer's food payments.

For any of these options, **LocalEats maximum cumulative benefit by the Gold milestone is R25 Bronze + R80 Gold = R105 per eligible customer**, if all granted and eventually fully redeemed. The percentage above is a ratio against **merchant gross food sales**, NOT LocalEats revenue or profit. A full customer journey continuing to Platinum could cost a maximum R255 at current proposed caps. These ratios are not evidence of actual margins or incremental purchases.

**Funding constraint:** Under the accepted R500 Bronze-only design, 12 first gifts potentially reserve R300 and R200 is buffer. If all 12 were also PROMISED R80 Gold credit, additional commitment would be R960 and cumulative Bronze+Gold commitments would total **R1,260**, even without Platinum. That violates the R500 ceiling. Therefore Gold's R80 is a **future feature only**, and should not appear as a guaranteed prize in the funded initial pilot.

## Credible evidence and limits

- Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2006, studied the goal-gradient effect with a café loyalty programme and other settings. Customers in the café visited more frequently as they approached a reward. This supports showing honest progress and a comprehensible next milestone, **not** a claim that ten kota units is experimentally optimal or that all redeemed rewards cause profitable retention: https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration-illusionary-goal
- Drèze & Nunes, *Journal of Marketing Research*, 2011, found goal achievement can increase effort on later attempts in another loyalty domain, with qualifications depending on task challenge. This supports investigating progressive milestones, but it does not establish South African kota demand: https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.48.2.268
- South African Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008, section 35, requires an offered loyalty award to be as represented with clear disclosure of the nature, qualifying steps, goods, providers, location and availability: https://api.acts.co.za/consumer-protection-act-2008/35_customer_loyalty_programmes.php. An exciting locked mission **may not** conceal the real requirements once the mission is offered, nor guarantee an unfunded R80 reward. Qualified legal review before public activation.

## Recommendation

**Try +10 qualifying paid-and-collected kota UNITS after the already-earned Bronze four as a first Gold design hypothesis, not yet approved.** The name/number is easy to understand: *Silver mission: Buy ten more eligible kotas and unlock Gold*. This is a full-ladder business concept for a later funded programme, not a new offer under the R500 pilot.

Do NOT require the ten kotas to be bought on different dates just to force another visit. The founder rejected that manipulation pattern for Bronze. Progress is trusted paid-and-collected **units**, with strict duplicates, cancellation/refund and free-unit exclusions. Promotion to Gold is once and rank permanent. An active, funded Gold mission must disclose the R80 credit and eligible terms before purchases are counted toward an advertised benefit.

## Evidence-first method without breaking the R500 cap

During the **Bronze-only 12-customer pilot, if separately authorised**, collect genuine paid-and-collected orders and unit counts for ordinary analytics. Quietly compute what proportion of actual customers **would have** bought 8, 10, 12 or 20 additional units after Bronze, together with separate paid visit count and repeat-after-gift outcomes. This is an internal **counterfactual threshold analysis**, not an actual Gold ledger, earnable mission, promised credit or automatic entitlement. Do not present an inactive Gold mission in the app as if customers can already earn R80.

The data should show distribution of real basket quantities, days between genuine paid visits, rate reaching thresholds, promo cost exposure and merchant participation. Even an observed high threshold completion rate cannot, by itself, demonstrate future incremental Gold-driven demand, since those shoppers were NOT offered Gold. When there is actual funding and legal/merchant approval, a later, transparently enrolled experiment can test the real programme's effect on retention.
